The engagement

Your team is the junior sell-side advisory team on this engagement. You work for the sellers, so your job is to get the owners the best deal. That means the best deal, which may not be the biggest number.

Re
Project Summit: sale of Summit Manufacturing, Inc.
Status
Two non-binding letters of intent (LOIs) received. Client decision needed this week.

Summit's co-founders, Mark Hansen and Dave Ortiz, have run the company for 15 years and are ready to retire. They put out a draft Confidential Information Memorandum (CIM) prepared by their internal team, which shows Adjusted EBITDA of $2.15 million. Two buyers responded with LOIs, and both based their price on that $2.15M figure.

Both LOIs are subject to each buyer's own Quality of Earnings review. Before we advise Mark and Dave, we need our own defensible view of earnings, value, and which offer to take. Deals move fast, so expect new information before the night is over.

What the owners told us matters, in order:

  1. Retire. Both want to be fully out of the business within about 12 months.
  2. Certainty. "We only want to go through this once." A deal that falls apart late would be very costly.
  3. Maximize what they actually receive, not the headline number.
  4. Take care of their people. Many of their 86 employees have been with them for over a decade.

What your team delivers

Adjusted EBITDA

Your supported view of what Summit earns, and whether that number holds under each buyer's terms.

Valuation

Pick an EBITDA multiple and a value range, and defend them.

Offer comparison

What each offer is worth to the owners today, including the 7:05 update.

Recommendation

Pick a buyer (or a counteroffer) and name 2–3 specific terms to negotiate.

Key risks

The top risks to value or to closing, and how to handle each one.

Presentation format: 5 minutes, any format. Slides, a one-pager, or a whiteboard all work. Speak to the judges as if they were Mark and Dave. Be ready for questions like "Why shouldn't we just take the highest offer?"

Exhibit E

Net working capital

Net working capital (NWC) = accounts receivable + inventory − accounts payable − accrued expenses.

Buyers expect a "normal" level of working capital to come with the business. The LOI sets a target (or "peg"). If NWC at closing is below the peg, the price goes down dollar for dollar. If it is above, the price goes up.

Both offers are cash-free, debt-free. Summit's $1.2M bank term loan is repaid from the sale proceeds under either offer, so it does not change your comparison.

How you'll be scored

Judging criteria

Hemming Morse professionals score every team on the same 100-point rubric. There is more than one defensible recommendation. Judges reward sound reasoning backed by the exhibits, not a single "right" answer.

Expect at least one tough question, such as:

  • Why shouldn't we just take the highest offer?
  • Which document supports that adjustment?
  • What does the update change, and what doesn't it change?
  • What would make you walk away from this deal?

Quick reference

M&A glossary

Sell-side advisor

The firm hired by the seller to run the sale process and get the best outcome. That is your team tonight.

EBITDA

Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. A rough measure of operating cash earnings that ignores how the company is financed.

Add-back / adjustment

An expense removed from EBITDA because it will not continue under a new owner (owner perks, one-time costs). Adjustments can also go the other way: a cost a new owner will have to pay lowers EBITDA.

Quality of Earnings (QoE)

A diligence review that tests whether reported earnings are real, recurring, and sustainable. Buyers always run their own.

Enterprise value (EV)

The value of the whole business before debt and cash. In small deals, EV is usually Adjusted EBITDA × a market multiple.

Multiple

How many years of EBITDA a buyer will pay. A higher multiple means lower perceived risk or stronger growth.

CIM

Confidential Information Memorandum. The detailed marketing document buyers receive after signing an NDA.

LOI

Letter of Intent. A mostly non-binding offer setting out price and key terms, usually with an exclusivity period.

Exclusivity

A period when the seller agrees not to negotiate with other buyers. Longer exclusivity shifts leverage to the buyer.

Re-trade

When a buyer lowers its offered price after receiving more information, often citing diligence findings.

Earnout

Part of the price paid later only if the business hits targets. It bridges a valuation gap but shifts risk to the seller.

Seller note

The seller lends part of the price to the buyer, who repays it over time. If it is subordinated, the bank gets paid first.

NWC peg

The target net working capital the buyer expects at closing. The price moves up or down by the difference.

Cash-free, debt-free

The seller keeps the cash and pays off the debt at closing, so EV converts to the seller's proceeds the same way in both offers.

Customer concentration

Dependence on a few large customers. Losing one could sharply cut earnings, so buyers apply a discount.

Present value

What a future payment is worth today: payment ÷ (1 + rate)years.
